== does UMA add over the free Gasteiger version? ==
  adding UMA on top of Gasteiger: F 24.3, p 4.55e-11
  correlation between uma_R and gast_R: +0.226

The low correlation matters: UMA is NOT reproducing the empirical charge
model, it is measuring something substantially different, and that something
adds signal on top.

== CHEMICALLY COHERENT PATTERN ==
The effect is weakest in carboxylic acids (655 rows, the LARGEST family) and
strongest in phenols. That is what the chemistry predicts: a carboxylate's
charge sits on its two oxygens almost regardless of substituent, so there is
little within-family variation in delocalization to detect. Phenolates vary
enormously (p-nitro vs p-methoxy), so there is a lot. The descriptor finds
signal exactly where chemical variation exists.

== STATISTICAL CAVEAT (important) ==
Rows are CLUSTERED BY MOLECULE: each molecule contributes up to 5 rows (one
per solvent), but the F-tests above treat rows as independent. The nominal
p-values are therefore anticonservative, roughly by the cluster size (~5).

Crude correction (divide F by ~5, refit dof to molecule counts):
  phenol    uma_F   333.9 -> 66.8   still p=2.7e-10  SURVIVES
  N-H acid  uma_IPR  47.3 ->  9.5   still p=4.5e-03  SURVIVES
  S-H acid  uma_IPR  58.8 -> 11.8   p=0.01           borderline
  C-H acid  uma_R    32.8 ->  6.6   p=0.03           does not survive
  carbox.   uma_F    11.9 ->  2.4   p=0.13           does not survive
  other O-H uma_IPR   8.8 ->  1.8   p=0.24           does not survive

So the honest claim is: the effect is established in PHENOLS and N-H ACIDS,
borderline in S-H, and not established elsewhere. A molecule-level
permutation test (now implemented, --n-perm) replaces this crude correction
with a valid p-value and MUST be run before any of this is quoted.

Other limitations: 7 descriptors x 6 families = 42 tests, so a multiple-
comparison correction is also needed; three families have <20 molecules;
and LOFO shows the descriptors do not transfer to an unseen family.
