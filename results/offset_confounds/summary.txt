== CHECK A: is the charge-type flip test runnable here? ==
unique reactions by charge type: {'neutral': 6487}
cationic acids (BH+ -> B + H+): 0 reactions

DEFINITIVE: zero of 6487 reactions are cationic acids. D2A-pKa is built from
HA >> A- by construction. The alpha(anionic) -> beta(cationic) sign-flip test
CANNOT be run on this dataset and needs cationic-acid pKa in the same solvents
(iBonD; the Leito basicity scales in MeCN/THF).

== THE LARGER EFFECT: within-solvent variation across families ==
per-solvent spread of the median shift ACROSS chemical families:
  Acetonitrile  11.22 to 17.03   5.81 pK
  DMF            2.10 to  8.15   6.05 pK
  DMSO           2.42 to  8.72   6.30 pK
  Ethanol        2.78 to  5.92   3.14 pK
  Methanol       2.05 to  5.56   3.51 pK

The between-solvent residual the decomposition explains is ~1.2-1.4 pK.
The WITHIN-solvent spread across families is 3-6 pK - several times larger.
A single pooled per-solvent offset is therefore a coarse summary of a broad
distribution, not a sharp constant. This is the solute-dependent transfer term
the cycle predicts, so it is expected, but it bounds what any per-solvent
constant can deliver.

== REPLICATION: the alpha dependence holds WITHIN each family separately ==
regressing (family offset - proton term) on d_alpha, per family:

family   n_solv    slope              95% CI     r2   rows
carb          5    -6.92      [-7.92, -5.92]  0.994    655
N-H           5    -6.41      [-7.52, -5.29]  0.991    172
phenol        5    -6.03      [-7.77, -4.29]  0.976    239
C-H           4    -2.84       [-8.12, 2.44]  0.728     74
othOH         4    -8.24     [-31.92, 15.44]  0.529     62
S-H                too few cells with >= 5 rows

Three chemically independent families (carboxylic acids, N-H acids, phenols)
each give slope -6.0 to -6.9 with r2 > 0.97 and non-overlapping-with-zero CIs,
on entirely different molecules. That is a genuine replication of the alpha
dependence, and it is stronger evidence than the single pooled 5-point fit:
the relationship is not an artifact of pooling heterogeneous chemistry.

TENTATIVE, UNDERPOWERED: the ordering carb (-6.92) > N-H (-6.41) >
phenol (-6.03) > C-H (-2.84) is the direction predicted by anion charge
delocalization (carboxylate charge localized on two oxygens; phenolate
delocalized into the ring; C-H acid anions such as enolates highly
delocalized -> least H-bond dependent). BUT the CIs overlap almost entirely,
and the C-H interval spans zero. The ordering is suggestive and in the
predicted direction; it is NOT established. Testing it properly needs more
solvents per family, or a continuous delocalization descriptor instead of
discrete families.
